# Phase 1 Autonomous Build Runbook

Status: active

Objective: continue from research to a state-of-the-art Alexander–Chioma arrival experience without restarting discovery between increments.

## Working rule

Read the minimum relevant canon, make the smallest complete scene increment, validate proportionately, commit directly to `main`, verify the production deployment and continue to the next wave.

Do not pause after routine discoveries or ask for confirmation when the answer is already locked in the canon.

## Stop only for

- a missing real-world fact that would otherwise be falsely published;
- image, music, portrait, vendor or cultural consent;
- recovery/approval of the final couple mark before brand lock;
- credentials or external spend not already authorized;
- a security/privacy decision that changes exposure;
- a genuine repository, CI or deployment failure after safe recovery attempts;
- two visual directions with materially different outcomes and no locked preference.

Everything else uses the documented fallback and continues.

## Wave 1 — Reference lock and atmospheric stills

Deliver:

- desktop and mobile concept still for every storyboard scene;
- lighting, material and negative-space proof;
- static/reduced-motion sequence;
- asset manifest with provenance.

Gate:

- one coherent world;
- no generic fantasy castle, neon, crowns or stock Afro-futurism;
- couple/date/action legible in five seconds;
- mobile compositions are independently authored.

## Wave 2 — Threshold vertical slice

Deliver Scene 1 and Scene 2 end to end:

- waiting envelope;
- recipient line;
- seal response;
- card rise;
- passage into garden;
- skip, reverse and reduced/static alternatives.

Gate:

- the guest perceives a change of place;
- focus and essential content never wait for animation;
- reverse scroll is coherent;
- context loss preserves position;
- reference phones remain responsive.

## Wave 3 — Story garden

Deliver the authored path, milestone clearings and proposal object using provisional content where necessary.

Gate:

- every object maps to a fact or approved placeholder;
- no generated faces;
- chapter-local loading and disposal work;
- one expressive moment per milestone.

## Wave 4 — Wedding circle

Deliver people, roles and vendors as part of the environment rather than a directory.

Gate:

- zero unapproved public identities;
- zero hover-only disclosure;
- zero-item, one-item, long-name and Unicode layouts pass.

## Wave 5 — Pavilion and details

Deliver the architectural arrival, date/place card, calendar, directions, dress guidance and safe sharing.

Gate:

- details remain crisp over every background state;
- utilities work with keyboard, touch and static mode;
- personalized sharing warns about social-cache persistence;
- crawler cards remain deterministic.

## Wave 6 — RSVP place setting

Deliver the complete simulated interaction first, then the real persistence slice only when Phase 4 authorizes it.

Gate:

- current simulation cannot imply delivery;
- later real success requires committed data;
- attendance, decline, dependent fields, error and retry are authored.

## Wave 7 — Atmosphere polish

Deliver optional sound, micro-reflections, restrained particles, environmental continuity and final resting compositions.

Gate:

- silent mode remains complete;
- no idle effect harms the performance budget;
- pause/mute and reduced motion are immediate;
- polish supports focus rather than competing with it.

## Wave 8 — Reference-device release

Test:

- 320, 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024 and 1440 CSS px;
- iOS Safari, Android Chrome and desktop Safari/Chrome/Firefox/Edge;
- reduced motion, no WebGL, context loss, 200% zoom and keyboard-only;
- public, personalized, invalid and revoked invitations;
- long names, Unicode, no people, many people and missing media;
- production OG images, calendar, sharing and privacy headers.

Release only after CI, production smoke and manual craft review are green.

## Commit rhythm

Each wave should produce a coherent commit and production checkpoint. A commit message names the guest-visible outcome, for example:

- `Establish the ceremony arrival art direction`
- `Build the envelope-to-garden threshold`
- `Open the story garden journey`
- `Reveal the glass pavilion`

Do not deploy meaningless formatting-only checkpoints during the visual build.

## Completion definition

Phase 1 is complete when a first-time guest can open the link on a phone and honestly describe the feeling as:

> “I entered their wedding.”

It is not complete when the implementation merely contains 3D objects, animation or a technically successful canvas.
